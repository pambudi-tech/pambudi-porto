# Behavior notes

Detailed notes on how each part of the portfolio looks and moves, with the modules that own it.

The bottom-center clock shows local time with seconds through enlarged dots on the same surface lattice. Its mobile placement clears the inward corner tails. It refreshes once per second and suspends while the page is hidden. Hovering the dove does not change it. Messages retain the dot style and wrap within the available space.

The black preloader uses Military Scribe from Adobe Web Project `ghx2tkl`. `src/pages/home.mjs` counts eight actual preparation tasks: the SVG, three decoded portfolio images, two loaded fonts, the application module, and the prepared scene. The image count comes from the DOM, including the preloader asset. `LoadingCounter` animates from 0% toward that real target with an ease-in-out curve, slowing at both ends. It never exceeds completed work or advances while its target is unchanged and already reached. Cached work animates across the 2000ms minimum; late resources get a short 360ms catch-up. Percentages count ready resources, not transferred bytes. Failed asset requests show a retry instead of being counted as loaded. Each request has a 20-second deadline that counts only time the page is drawn, so a background tab never times out. `PRELOADER` in `src/preloader/loading-progress.mjs` shares the minimum, catch-up, 120ms completion hold, 320ms exit, and 1000ms welcome ink duration. The welcome reuses `paperInkEntrance` and its ink layers, preserving connected script lettering. Page entrances begin with the exit fade. `PAPER_PLACEMENT` in `src/paper/paper-motion.mjs` controls a 900ms placement at 100% scale, with a slight drop, tilt, and shadow settling; it composes with the existing paper interaction and ends at the original resting pose. Reduced motion skips the animated placement and ink growth.

The counter uses a quintic ease-in-out curve, with a stronger slowdown near completion. Its final 97%, 98%, 99%, and 100% appear in order, pausing for 100ms, 180ms, and 300ms respectively (`PRELOADER.finalSteps`), even when a long frame would otherwise skip a value. It can show a step only after real progress has reached it.

`src/clock/departure-board.mjs` owns the reusable character transition and its shared `DEPARTURE_MOTION` timing. - `src/stitch/motif-data.mjs` contains the traced charts; the original references are in `tools/motif-references/`. `python3 tools/trace-motifs.py` regenerates the data using measured grid centers and a small palette per reference to remove compression noise. The first red embroidery photo supplies the thread-style direction; the rose and dinosaur use their later replacement images.
- `src/stitch/motifs.mjs` maps the source cells to the surface and staggers their stitch reveal from the center outward. A shuffled bag serves all nine motifs before reshuffling and prevents consecutive repeats. One motif is one undo action, including overlaps. Each motif automatically unpicks 30 seconds after placement, working from the outside inward with the reverse thread animation. Expiry preserves newer overlapping motifs and free stitches, and removes the expired placement from undo/redo history. Templates map directly onto the shared lattice without rotation or resampling. Motifs sew below the paper and preserve anchored ornament stitches. The duck, watermelon, and rose use brighter thread palettes in `src/stitch/motifs.mjs`; the other six retain their traced colors.
- Right-click or long-press for the white stitch panel, with a Motif / Free stitch mode switch and undo/redo. Shift F10 or the context-menu key opens it with a keyboard. Dismissing the panel does not sew another stitch. The sage text-selection highlight is independent of the panel colors.
- Free stitch adds individual X stitches with a click, tap, or drag, using black by default. Fast strokes fill skipped grid cells; one complete stroke is one undo action. Touch waits to distinguish a tap, drag, and long-press. Drawing protects the paper and anchored ornament stitches, and crossing the paper breaks the stroke connection. Free stitches share the motif reveal layer and reduced-motion behavior through `src/stitch/free-stitch.mjs`.
- The Free stitch palette offers blue, yellow, red, green, purple, and black. A single Unpick toggle switches removal on or off. Unpick removes individual X stitches, including visitor motifs and anchored ornaments, by reversing their thread reveal beneath the paper. Each removal stroke remains one undo action and restores the exact previous colors. Choosing a color turns Unpick off. The last color and action persist while switching modes during the visit. `src/stitch/thread-colors.mjs` supplies shared colors; the bird's red uses the exact same `#c6434b` as the Red swatch.
- `src/stitch/stitch-panel.mjs` owns reusable panel motion through `STITCH_PANEL_MOTION`: a 240ms response with `cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)`, shared with CSS as duration/easing tokens. Entry and exit ease opacity, position, and scale; the same persistent mode pill slides between options. The palette section expands and fades while preserving the panel's anchor side. Switching modes keeps the panel open. Closing makes it inert immediately, delays hiding until its exit finishes, and cancels that hide if reopened. Reduced motion makes these transitions immediate.
- Dragging, cancelled pointers, long-presses, and multi-touch do not place motifs. Paper and wax-seal presses keep their existing interaction.
- Hover uses the original faint X and four stitch-hole rings, snapping from cell to cell on the shared surface grid. Clicking still places a random motif at that cell.
- The surface has no pan or zoom controls or gestures. Corner motifs stay anchored to the page edges as the viewport changes. Visitor motifs retain ordinary integer lattice coordinates so a resize cannot split them across corner slots.
- A bird, reconstructed from the supplied cross-stitch animation, travels through the surface's actual dot grid. Its pattern is sampled at the same stitch spacing as the corner motifs and visitor stitches. Each X sews and withdraws in place as the bird changes cells; it is drawn on the embroidery canvas. Permanent embroidery keeps its cells. The bird never changes visitor embroidery or adds animation frames to undo history.
- The bird wanders instead of following a fixed line. `src/bird/flight-path.mjs` steers its center toward changing targets with smoothly changing speed, so every change of course is a curve, and mixes cruising, faster-flapping climbs, glides and swoops with the wings held at the top of the stroke, wide laps round the paper, and occasional exits. After at least 14 seconds on screen it may leave, likeliest when already heading out at an edge, and return 1.2–3.6 seconds later from either edge at a new height. Legs carry on the way it faces, usually across the paper to the open cloth on the far side, sometimes a shorter hop; it turns back only when there is little room before the edge, so it never dithers on one side. It hurries across behind the paper and slows on open cloth. It never rises or falls without travelling across: no climb or dive is steeper than `FLIGHT.maxSlope` (0.6 rad), and turning back is a level turn in which it slows, faces the other way and sets off again. It faces the way it flies, and climbs and dives pitch the whole figure by up to 0.3 rad about the breast. Wingbeats and body follow-through run on the flight's wing clock. Shared `FLIGHT` values set speeds, turn rates and timing; narrow screens fly slower. Each visit uses a new random seed, and the same seed flies the same path. The figure is two thirds of the source chart (`FIGURE_SCALE`). It flies between the top of the screen and the row above the clock, behind the paper and the wordmark: wordmark holes stay reserved, so no hole holds two threads.
- On first load, the bird reveals from its center outward with the shared irregular flame pattern. The reveal follows its flight and wing poses. All stitches retain their original red throughout the flight.
- The bird pauses when reduced motion is preferred and suspends while the page is hidden.
